Co-op

Eight players, one world

Not eight copies of the same map — one shared world, one set of creatures, one ecosystem. What another player kills stays dead. What they leave behind stays there.

Join in seconds

Install and play

The APK already knows the server. No account, no settings, no GPU on your device — the world is computed for you and streamed as it changes.

When it is busy

You wait in a queue

If all eight seats are taken, the game shows your position in line and lets you in the moment someone leaves. Host downtime is reported separately from a full world.

Your character

You keep what you are

Characters are saved, including your grafts. Upgrade the app in place and you keep the same character — uninstalling or clearing app storage loses it.

Straight answers

What works, and what we have not proven

This is an early demo. We would rather tell you the limits than have you find them.

  • Eight seats, queueing, saves. Enforced by the live server and tested with players joining, waiting and being admitted.
  • Eight-player load is measured on a local rig, not over the open internet. The four-player measurement over the real network came in at 247 ms against a 250 ms budget, so busy-world performance at eight is not yet proven in the wild.
  • macOS is unverified. The Mac launcher exists but has never been run on a physical Mac.
  • A fresh Windows installation has not been fully verified end to end.
  • The world runs on one PC belonging to the developer, so the demo is online when that machine is. It is not a continuously available service, and there is no schedule or uptime promise.
  • If the host is down you will see that separately from a full server — you are not stuck in a queue for an offline world.

What this is

A world that keeps its own books

Deepwarren is a co-operative survival crawl through a fungal underworld. The creatures, the factions and the roaming commanders are run by a simulation that keeps going between your visits — it remembers, it decays, it spreads, and it changes what waits for you next time.

The demo shows one floor of it. What you see on this page is built from the game's own art files, frame for frame — including the animations, which are the real sprite rows the game plays.
