What this is

A world that keeps its own books.

Deepwarren is a co-operative survival crawl through a fungal underworld. The creatures, the factions and the roaming commanders run on a simulation that keeps going between your visits. It remembers, it decays, it spreads, and it changes what waits for you next time.

01 · Ecosystem

Nothing waits for you

Creatures eat, hunt, graze, flee and dig on their own. A spider on the far side of the map keeps living while you walk back to camp.

02 · Bodies

Rebuild yourself

Head, torso and each limb can be replaced with parts taken from what you fight. Spider fangs, raptor forelimbs, hare hindlegs.

03 · Shared world

One world, eight people

Not eight copies of a map. What another player kills stays dead, and what they leave behind stays there.

Battle

Aim for the leg. It will aim for yours.

Fights are turn-based and physical. You pick which part to strike, and the creature does the same. A part beaten to zero turns purple-black and stops working: a dead leg slows you, a dead arm weakens your blows. It mends over time, or you can bandage it mid-fight. Losing does not always mean dying.

Grafting

Wear what you take.

Parts taken from creatures can be bonded onto your own body at camp or at a settlement's graft bench. A professional bench guarantees a full-strength bond. A surgeon can also move your head onto a new body. Your name, class, skills, possessions and memories stay with the head, while stats, senses and anatomy follow the body. The Chitinborn, with four working arms, are playable.

Below the hollows

The Chitin Galleries.

The next floor down is an ant colony that farms: nursery courts, brood vaults, a queen's chamber and aphid folds worked by the galleries' own castes. In this demo, the guarded descent at the east end of the Hollows is where your run finishes. Beat the Dusk-Eyed Wolf and the way down opens.

Music

An orchestra from a sound card.

The score is written for the General MIDI orchestra of 80s and 90s SNES and DOS games: strings, brass, timpani and harp, all thin and bright the way that hardware sounded. Every place and every fight has its own cue. These are the tracks that ship in the game.

Co-op

Eight players, one world.

One shared world, one set of creatures, one ecosystem. The world is computed on the server and streamed to you as it changes, so your device does not need a GPU.

8

Join in seconds

Install and play

The APK already knows the server. There is no account and nothing to configure.

When it is busy

You wait in a queue

If all eight seats are taken, the game shows your place in line and lets you in when someone leaves. Host downtime is reported separately from a full world.

Your character

You keep what you are

Characters are saved, grafts included. Upgrade the app in place and you keep them. Uninstalling or clearing app storage loses them.

Straight answers

What works, and what we have not proven.

This is an early demo. We would rather tell you the limits than have you find them.

  • Eight seats, queueing and saves are enforced by the live server and tested with players joining, waiting and being admitted.
  • Eight-player load was measured on a local rig, not over the open internet. Four players over the real network came in at 247 ms against a 250 ms budget, so eight in the wild is not yet proven.
  • macOS is unverified. The Mac launcher exists but has never been run on a physical Mac.
  • A fresh Windows install has not been fully verified end to end.
  • The world runs on the developer's own PC. The demo is online when that machine is. There is no schedule or uptime promise.
  • If the host is down, you are told so. You are never left waiting in a queue for an offline world.
